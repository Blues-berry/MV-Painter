# CAI predictive-value decision

**Final state: `DESCRIPTIVE_ONLY`.**

## Prospective prediction audit

No CAI-based schedule prediction was frozen before an independent outcome
cohort and then tested prospectively. The schedule evidence instead shows
metric-dependent layer/window effects, counterexamples to a unique CAI
selector, and a set-valued or undefined frozen CAI rule on MV-Adapter. Those
observations are useful for describing measured stage utilities, but do not
establish predictive value beyond the outcomes used to construct the
interpretation.

## Evidence and limits

- The current manuscript already calls CAI an empirical diagnostic, says it
  is not a theorem or guaranteed selector, and states that it does not select
  a unique schedule (`final_round2.tex`, §3.5, `subsec:cai`).
- The response to Reviewer 2.2 directly acknowledges that CAI is
  post-hoc/interpretive, that the factorial offers counterexamples to a
  unique schedule prediction, and that the frozen rule is set-valued or
  undefined on MV-Adapter (`response_letter_round2.md`, R2.2).
- A3b and the new independent cohort were designed to test bounded
  layer-window effects, not a CAI prediction. They cannot retroactively turn
  the prior description into a prospective selector.

## Claim disposition

Do not describe CAI as a predictive framework, a schedule-selection method,
or a separate innovation. It may remain as a concise empirical vocabulary
for measured stage utilities if that description helps interpret the
results. Any later manuscript rewrite should reduce the CAI subsection and
remove it from the contributions/novelty claims; retain the set-valued
MV-Adapter result and the post-hoc limitation.

No new experiment is justified solely to preserve CAI as a contribution.
