# Response to Reviewers — Layer-wise Revision (next-round working package)

## Editorial note

We thank all reviewers. This revision repositions the paper around
**layer-wise stage-aware adapter scaling**: a training-free inference control
that assigns each UNet depth group its own stage-dependent residual scale,
$h'_{l,t}=h_{l,t}+s_l(p_t)A_l(h_{l,t},G)$, with per-group implementation caps
(deep 3.0, middle 3.5, shallow 0.8) and no change to any network weight. The
shared-layer TCAS/C3 schedule is retained as a baseline rather than as the
claimed optimum. New evidence since the previous package: (i) a complete
binary enumeration of all eight temporal patterns on the 24-object probe with
three seeds (576 paired records); (ii) a pre-registered strict-276-object
confirmation of the development-selected pattern under a single runner;
(iii) explicit runner-sensitivity documentation; and (iv) patched Figure 1
and rewritten method/limitations sections. All legacy numbers (+0.96 dB,
CLIP-IQA, preference study) are removed from the deliverable source, not just
commented out; the previous manuscript is archived unmodified.

## Reviewer 1

### R1.1 — Full-object quality, color shifts, repeated textures, baking, unseen views, seams, video

**Direct answer.** We agree that earlier qualitative evidence was
insufficient and partly misleading. The revision keeps three kinds of
complete-object evidence, all generated by the same frozen pipeline with the
same reference and views per object: (a) complete-object panels for a
representative improvement, a near tie, and failure-boundary objects
(obj\_0048, obj\_0078) including the unmodified pipeline (no adapter) and a
competitive fixed-low baseline; (b) the full 12-object contact sheet
(Supplementary S4a) — the whole stratified baking cohort, not a success-only
selection; (c) a CPU bake with 48 exported textured GLBs and 528 unseen-view
renders (11 unseen views per object–condition).

**What the 3D evidence does and does not show.** Two bake evidence layers
are reported, and neither is a population-level quality claim.

1. *Original global case study (unchanged scope).* Four global conditions
   (no adapter, fixed-low, fixed-high, C3) on the stratified cohort: masked
   PSNR 11.824 (no adapter) vs 6.001 (fixed-low) vs 5.638 (C3); CIEDE2000
   and FG-LPIPS order the same way. No C3 advantage; visual inspection found
   cross-view source-fusion colour inconsistency; obj\_0048 has 0.8441
   inpainted fraction and exporter vertex reindexing; obj\_0078/obj\_0082
   have raw coverage below 0.04.
2. *New layer-wise bake extension (same-draw controls).* The layer-wise
   schedules were baked on the same 12 Exact-GLB objects under the same
   bake protocol, together with fresh global fixed-low/C3 controls generated
   by the SAME seeded runner (same per-object reference draws), because the
   reference-preprocessing draw shifts absolute values between runners.
   Within this same-draw set the four means are strictly ordered — global
   fixed-low 10.763 dB / global C3 12.565 / layer-LHL 15.005 / layer-LLH
   16.679 masked PSNR (CIEDE2000 26.287 / 21.643 / 16.922 / 14.186; FG-LPIPS
   0.1705 / 0.1562 / 0.1420 / 0.1215) — and layer-LLH beats layer-LHL and
   global C3 with 12/12 paired object wins on all three metrics, including
   the hard cases obj\_0048, obj\_0078, obj\_0082. This is the first 3D
   evidence for the layer-wise family; it is still a 12-object stratified
   case study (no GT bake, no DISTS) and is labeled as such.

**Seams and video.** The seam scalar saved by the bake audit has no seam-pair
denominator, so we report the zero seam values with their definition and
explicitly do not treat them as seam-free proof (Supplementary S4). The
supplementary video: no video is part of this revision package, and we no
longer reference one anywhere in the manuscript.

**Changes.** Baking section and Limitations rewritten around this boundary;
Supplementary S4a retains the complete contact sheet.

### R1.2 — Set crossing, strict holdout for the main adapter, the historical +0.96 dB

**Direct answer.** Three separate actions were taken.

1. *The historical +0.96 dB is retired.* The original v1 checkpoint is lost,
   so the number can neither be re-verified nor reproduced; the historical
   pool also overlapped the probe objects. It appears nowhere in the
   deliverable source (the old table block is deleted, not commented), and
   the claim ledger records it as HISTORICAL\_ONLY. We describe the current
   clean-v2 protocol as a *controlled retraining* evaluation, not a recovery
   of the original checkpoint.
2. *Same-held-out-protocol comparison for the main adapter.* The strict-276
   clean-v2 panel evaluates no-adapter, fixed-low, fixed-high, and C3 with
   one checkpoint and one seed. It is a non-dominance result: fixed-low beats
   C3 on Full/FG-PSNR and FG-SSIM; C3 is non-inferior on FG-LPIPS/Edge-SSIM
   descriptively. We claim no blanket improvement over fixed-low.
3. *Layer-wise holdout evidence.* The strict-276 layer-LHL record is labeled
   an independent transfer record. Because the development probes selected
   layer-LLH, we additionally ran a pre-registered strict-276 confirmation
   (protocol frozen before any holdout number was observed; candidates
   chosen only on probe FG-LPIPS) under one runner with per-object seeding:
   all seven metrics favor layer-LLH over layer-fixed-mean with intervals
   excluding zero (e.g., Full-PSNR +1.311 dB [+1.219,+1.400], 275/276 wins;
   FG-LPIPS −0.0089 [−0.0097,−0.0081], 258/276 wins; FG-PSNR +0.875 dB,
   Edge-SSIM +0.0113, Full-LPIPS −0.0094, Full-SSIM +0.0011, FG-SSIM +0.0070,
   all 95% CIs excluding zero). The layer-LHL same-runner replica loses to
   layer-LLH on all seven metrics (Full-PSNR +1.193 dB [+1.123,+1.262],
   276/276 wins; FG-PSNR +1.001 dB, 261/276; FG-LPIPS −0.0110, 264/276;
   Edge-SSIM +0.0203) and is mixed against layer-fixed-mean (fixed-mean
   better on FG-LPIPS/FG-PSNR/Edge-SSIM, replica better on Full-PSNR),
   matching the metric-dependent development picture. The replica's absolute
   means are lower than the archived unseeded layer-LHL record (FG-PSNR
   12.97 vs 14.78, Pearson r=0.66 over objects), which quantifies the
   reference-preprocessing draw sensitivity between runners; the archived
   record is retained as a single unseeded run, and all paired conclusions
   come from the seeded same-runner set. Probe
   selection and holdout confirmation are kept separate; no parameter was
   re-tuned on the holdout.

### R1.3 — Texture variation vs texture fidelity

**Direct answer.** The revision separates four evaluation dimensions
explicitly (manuscript "Shape-Texture Diagnostic Metrics"): structure
(FG/Edge/Crop-SSIM), reference fidelity (PSNR, masked LPIPS, GT-relative
errors of the variation diagnostics), texture variation (Laplacian variance,
RGB std, gradient magnitude, HF energy — descriptive only), and actual 3D
output quality. The text now states that larger gradients or higher HF energy
can equally reflect unwanted noise, repeated patterns, or color shifts, and
no claim in the paper derives fidelity from variation alone; variation
statements are always paired with GT-relative errors or visual
counterexamples (the failure-boundary panels double as counterexamples:
purple shift and repeated patterns occur with locally high high-frequency
response). For the layer-wise transfer record, the GT-relative texture
diagnostics are reported with paired CIs (e.g., RGB-std error −0.0152
[−0.0178,−0.0126] and HF-energy error −0.0608 [−0.0655,−0.0559] vs
fixed-low; gradient-error reduction reaches zero, CI
[−0.0401,+0.0001], and is described as a trend, not a decisive claim).

### R1.4 — Paired CIs, Edge-SSIM trade-off, CI crossing zero ≠ equivalence

**Direct answer.** All active tables report object-level paired 95%
percentile bootstrap intervals (10,000 resamples, fixed seed) with win
rates. The statistical-reporting paragraph states that an interval crossing
zero is "no detected difference", never equivalence or non-inferiority, and
the paper claims no non-inferiority margin anywhere. The historical
Edge-SSIM 0.529 vs 0.559 comparison belonged to the retired historical
table; the current strict-276 panel reports Edge-SSIM with paired CIs, and
where the schedule loses (e.g., C3-minus-LLH saved-artifact Full-SSIM
−0.00170 [−0.00205,−0.00134]) the loss is stated as a detected loss.

### R1.5 — FAC implementation and code reproducibility

**Direct answer.** The manuscript now documents the FAC re-examination
sufficiently to re-implement: three modules (LTAG/GSG/FSC, ~6×10³
parameters), the training objective (latent noise-prediction MSE with 1.7×
foreground / 1.3× edge reweighting plus a latent-SSIM term), AdamW
(peak LR 10⁻⁴, 100 warm-up steps, 2,000 steps), the disjoint 1,706-object
strong-residual training pool, and the paired evaluation protocol. Code and
per-object records are available from the corresponding author on reasonable
request; the Data availability statement lists the exact artifact classes
(evaluation scripts, per-object records, protocol manifests, hash indexes)
and their license constraints. We do not promise a public repository URL in
the manuscript because none is registered yet; the request-based statement
is the accurate one.

## Reviewer 2

### R2.1 — Contribution beyond schedule selection for an existing adapter

**Direct answer.** We agree the previous version read as "a three-stage
schedule found by search". The contribution is now stated and tested as
**training-free layer-wise adapter residual scaling with empirical
stage-dependent trade-offs**, which changes the object of study in three
ways.

1. *Why depth groups are independent control variables.* Residuals are
   injected at three up-blocks with different spatial resolution and roles
   (global structure / shape / fine texture); the implementation applies
   per-group caps (3.0/3.5/0.8), so a global scale is already an implicit
   layer allocation. The method makes this explicit: $s_l(p)$ per group.
2. *Shared-input ablation separates the effects.* With per-object shared
   targets/features/latents: layer redistribution alone (layer-fixed-low
   vs global fixed-low) improves foreground fidelity; constant vs scheduled
   (layer-fixed-mean vs layer-LHL) and placement (8-pattern factorial) are
   then measured on top. The complete factorial shows robust directions —
   late-high helps, early-high hurts — and explicit counterexamples: layer-
   LLH, layer-LHH, and constant layer-LLL all beat layer-LHL on FG-LPIPS.
3. *No unique-optimum claim.* We do not claim layer-LHL (or any pattern) is
   uniquely optimal, universally superior, or CAI-derived. CAI is an
   empirical diagnostic; on the second backbone the frozen rule is
   undefined/set-valued.

### R2.2 — Is CAI post-hoc interpretation?

**Direct answer.** Yes — we state this. CAI is used as an empirical stage-
utility description, not a derivation: the manuscript paragraph "How the
stage schedule is selected" and the Remark define it as an empirical
diagnostic whose additive stage decomposition is only an approximation, and
the new factorial confirms it has no unique predictive power (a rule that
predicted the mid-stage high scale would be best is contradicted by
late-high patterns winning). The frozen CAI rule on MV-Adapter is
undefined/set-valued. CAI appears in no contribution statement.

### R2.3 — Cross-backbone validation

**Direct answer.** The second-backbone evidence remains a *global
stage-position replication*, and we now say so in the section title context,
the text, and Limitations. Concretely: the MV-Adapter deployment applies one
scalar to all injected residuals; the injected residuals are technically a
per-block list that could be scaled element-wise, but a layer-wise mapping
would require a depth-group allocation, a recalibrated low/high pair, and a
full 76-object rerun, which we have not done. Within the global audit,
paired differences are metric-specific (LHL's Edge-SSIM advantage
+0.000613 [+0.000135,+0.001217] over HLL; LLH highest PSNR and lowest
GT-relative texture error), the frozen CAI rule is undefined/set-valued,
official pretraining UID disjointness is unknown, and absolute scores are
never compared across backbones. The MVDiffusion deployment remains an
interface diagnostic and is never used as a quantitative second backbone.
