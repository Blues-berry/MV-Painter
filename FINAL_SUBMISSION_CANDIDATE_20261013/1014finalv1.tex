\title{Timestep-Conditioned Adapter Scaling for Multi-view Diffusion Texture Generation: Control Allocation and Baked Appearance}

% Double-anonymized review: author names, affiliations, and acknowledgements
% (including funding) are provided only on the separately uploaded title page.

\begin{abstract}
Geometry-conditioned multi-view diffusion produces images that are baked onto 3D meshes, but the interpretation of a geometry-control schedule depends on its implemented strength and the evaluated endpoint. We study Timestep-Conditioned Adapter Scaling (TCAS) on an existing GeoTex residual path, separating requested scale, native-cap-adjusted applied scale and state-dependent residual correction. On 300 held-out objects, the historical low--high--low C3 profile gains $0.522$ dB foreground PSNR over Fixed Low while increasing the applied budget; Fixed High and Generic Linear have better means on all four image endpoints. At matched applied-scale sums, the specified Middle-high minus average(Early-high,Late-high) contrast does not detect a middle-window advantage ($-0.0339$ dB, 95\% interval $[-0.1172,+0.0514]$, $p=0.4324$). Actual native-UV baking yields a small Late-high foreground LPIPS benefit on ten compatible GeoTex objects ($-0.00265$), which is not detected in a separate twenty-object GSO study. Native MV-Adapter comparisons likewise do not detect the specified middle-window gain. The findings change the interpretation of C3 and identify practical evaluation requirements: competitive controls, matching at the implemented interface, and unseen-view assessment of the resulting asset. They establish task-specific allocation effects and limits, without a universal schedule or faithful material reproduction.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Multi-view diffusion is an intermediate step in many 3D texturing systems: a reference image and geometry guide the generation of RGB views, which are then projected or baked onto a mesh. The graphics target is the textured asset viewed from new cameras. A useful inference control must therefore improve surface appearance under this complete workflow, rather than only a selected score on the generated images.

Geometry strength is a readily accessible control, but its numerical setting can obscure what an experiment changes. A temporal profile compared with a weaker constant also changes total strength. Existing wrapper caps may clip a requested profile differently at different injection sites. Even after matching the applied scalar sums, the correction tensors depend on the evolving hidden state. Consequently, a better score for a scheduled run does not by itself identify a benefit of its temporal placement.

The downstream endpoint adds a second distinction. Projection, coverage, resampling and agreement between incoming views affect the appearance of a baked texture. A generated-view improvement can remain small relative to reference-appearance error, and a texture-variation statistic can increase because of artifacts. These implementation and endpoint distinctions matter when deciding whether to adopt a geometry-control setting in a multi-view texturing system.

We investigate these questions using Timestep-Conditioned Adapter Scaling (TCAS), a requested scale profile on an existing GeoTex-Adapter residual path. The recorded C3 profile requests $(1.25,2.50,1.25)$ over the early, middle and late sampling windows. Conditioning schedules already exist: ControlNet exposes layer weights and temporal gates, and Scheduled Style Injection (SSI) schedules both attention-based style mixing and geometric ControlNet conditioning \cite{controlnet2023layercontrol,diffusers2023controlnet,kulkarni2026ssi}. Our research question concerns what controlled comparisons can establish for multi-view texturing after native clipping and asset construction. C3 is evaluated as a fixed operating point, without another schedule search or a claim of a new general scheduling principle.

The study links three questions: does C3 remain advantageous against competitive fixed and linear controls; what does moving the same high-scale segment establish at matched applied budgets; and how do these differences appear in actual UV-textured assets and other native conditioning interfaces? The answers revise the original interpretation of the profile. C3 improves over Fixed Low on the held-out GeoTex cohort, while stronger controls have better image-endpoint means. The specified equal-budget primary does not support a middle-window advantage. Baking reveals a small local benefit for a different allocation, together with substantial appearance errors and a separate-protocol non-detection. Different conditioning interfaces also fail to establish a stable middle-window gain.

The supported contributions are:

(1) \textbf{An implementation-aware characterization of geometry-control allocation.} Requested, capped applied and realized residual quantities are distinguished, and competitive controls change the interpretation of the C3 gain. Matched per-group scalar sums test temporal placement without treating them as matched residual doses. The non-detected primary and separate pairwise effects constrain the earlier middle-window recommendation.

(2) \textbf{A measured connection between view-level control and baked appearance.} Native-UV meshes, matched-resolution ground-truth self-bakes and eleven unseen cameras quantify the size of allocation changes relative to persistent asset-appearance error. Positive image/bake rank associations coexist with changing point-estimate winners; a small local LPIPS gain does not establish faithful material reproduction or a benefit across the two bake protocols.

(3) \textbf{Evidence delimiting transfer across conditioning interfaces.} Native MV-Adapter and retained MVDiffusion studies test related allocation questions within their actual operators and camera contracts. Their small or endpoint-dependent responses limit schedule transfer and show why common profile labels cannot be assumed to encode a common intervention.

For graphics researchers, these findings affect both comparison design and deployment decisions: a schedule should be assessed against strong settings at the implemented control site, and its practical value should be measured on the resulting textured mesh. The evidence supports these bounded decisions without requiring a universally winning profile.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{3D Texture Generation and Multi-view Diffusion}
\label{subsec:3dtex}

Single-image 3D generation is often decomposed into geometry generation and texture generation. Single-image-to-3D, multi-view diffusion, and sparse-view reconstruction methods have improved structural stability and view synthesis \cite{liu2023zero1to3,shi2023zero123pp,liu2023syncdreamer,long2023wonder3d,shi2024mvdream,wang2023imagedream,xu2024instantmesh,ke2024era3d}. Texture synthesis remains a distinct challenge for asset realism and usability \cite{perla2026survey}. Many systems generate RGB views with 2D diffusion and project or bake them onto a surface \cite{chen2023text2tex,richardson2023texture,poole2023dreamfusion,chen2023fantasia3d}. Recent work also explores exemplar-based texture synthesis and diffusion-based procedural materials \cite{guzman2026adaptive,lv2025interpretable}.

Paint3D, MVPaint, Hunyuan3D 2.0, and MVPainter use geometry-conditioned multi-view generation in 3D texturing pipelines \cite{zeng2024paint3d,cheng2024mvpaint,lai2025hunyuan3d,shao2025mvpainter}. MV-Adapter, MVDiffusion, and other synchronized multi-view methods address consistency through different adapter and denoising designs \cite{huang2024mvadapter,tang2023mvdiffusion,liu2024synchronized,xu2026wondertex}. These systems establish the graphics context for this work. The present evidence does not compare final baked-texture quality across those systems.

\subsection{Adapter-Augmented Diffusion Models and Geometric Control}
\label{subsec:adapter}

ControlNet and T2I-Adapter provide learned spatial-conditioning branches or feature injections, whose contribution can be weighted by an inference-time condition-strength scale \cite{mou2023t2iadapter,zhang2023controlnet}. They establish conditioning strength as a practical control, but their injected tensors and scaling sites do not share a numerical dose with GeoTex. The studied GeoTex-Adapter path already exposes an inference-time residual scale and evaluates fixed requests. TCAS reuses that control point as a three-stage request over sampling progress, while recording how wrapper-group caps change the applied values and how adapter activations determine the resulting corrections. It adds no learned branch or schedule selector. IP-Adapter introduces image prompts through decoupled attention \cite{ye2023ipadapter}; LoRA and related methods use scaled low-rank updates \cite{hu2022lora,liu2024dora,zhang2023adalora}.

\subsection{Diffusion Guidance Scheduling and Timestep Control}
\label{subsec:guidance}

Diffusion denoising is often discussed in terms of coarse-to-fine evolution \cite{ho2020ddpm,song2021ddim}. Classifier-free guidance combines conditional and unconditional score predictions \cite{ho2022cfg}; limiting it to an interval studies quality and diversity \cite{kynkanniemi2024limited}. Adaptive Guidance instead schedules conditional/unconditional evaluations for inference efficiency \cite{castillo2023adaptive}. These score-space and computation schedules differ from the geometry-residual intervention tested here.

Earlier official ControlNet code already exposes layer-dependent control weights, and Diffusers v0.21.0 documents temporal conditioning start/end gates \cite{controlnet2023layercontrol,diffusers2023controlnet}. SSI is the closest combined layer/time scheduling precedent. It varies StyleID's query-mixing parameter \cite{chung2024styleid} across decoder layers and denoising time, tests schedule direction and shape, and also schedules a separate geometry-conditioned ControlNet branch \cite{kulkarni2026ssi}. Thus, layer/time scheduling and geometry-conditioning schedules are established ideas. TCAS acts on the residual correction produced by GeoTex-Adapter from normal, depth, and foreground-mask conditions, with group-specific wrapper caps. In this 3D texturing setting, we measure how that intervention relates to generated-view agreement, reference-color errors, and baked appearance. SSI evaluates image style/content trade-offs. Our controlled question connects an implemented geometry-residual allocation to generated views and native-UV assets. The differences in task, operator and endpoints motivate this evaluation; they do not establish novelty of temporal or layer scheduling itself.

\subsection{Multi-view Adapter Interfaces}
\label{subsec:mvinterfaces}

MV-Adapter uses a condition guider for camera or geometry maps together with decoupled spatial, multi-view, and optional image cross-attention layers \cite{huang2024mvadapter}. MVDiffusion uses correspondence-aware attention modules to connect pixels across views \cite{tang2023mvdiffusion}. These interfaces differ from the GeoTex-Adapter residual branch studied here. We therefore report each auxiliary intervention within its own tested implementation and cohort, without pooling metrics or interpreting it as an architecture-matched replication of TCAS. The native temporal comparisons are summarized in Tables~\ref{tab:new-mva} and \ref{tab:interface-effects}; historical panels remain in Supplementary Section~S3.

\section{Control Formulation and Study Design}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Task Definition and Base Pipeline}
\label{subsec:task}

We study inference-time geometry strength in an existing MVPainter-style multi-view texturing framework \cite{shao2025mvpainter}. Reference conditioning follows the base image path; normal, depth and mask conditions enter GeoTex-Adapter. TCAS sets the requested multiplier on the existing residual branch. The existing wrapper caps that value before multiplication and addition to frozen diffusion U-Net features (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}). The base U-Net, VAE and adapter weights remain frozen. Fresh C evaluates six generated RGB views; separately identified bake cohorts assess actual UV-textured meshes from eleven unseen cameras. Baking is a downstream evaluation, not a module introduced by TCAS.

During multi-view diffusion, the 3D geometry is rendered into normal maps, depth maps, and foreground masks, denoted as:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{G} = \{G_\text{normal}, G_\text{depth}, G_\text{mask}\}.
\end{equation}

The geometric adapter maps these conditions to a residual correction term and injects it into the intermediate hidden states of the UNet. For inference step $j$ with normalized execution progress $p_j$, the adapter injection can be written as:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}'_{j,\ell} = \mathbf{h}_{j,\ell} + s_{\mathrm{app}}(p_j,\ell)\, A^\ell_\phi(\mathbf{h}_{j,\ell}, \mathbf{G}),
\label{eq:injection}
\end{equation}
Here $\ell$ identifies a wrapper within one of the three injection groups; caps and requested profiles are assigned by group. The \emph{requested scale} $s_{\mathrm{req}}(p_j,\ell)$ is the value assigned by the profile; the \emph{applied scale} is the value used by the wrapper,
\begin{equation}
s_{\mathrm{app}}(p_j,\ell)=\min\!\bigl(s_{\mathrm{req}}(p_j,\ell),c_\ell\bigr),
\end{equation}
when the native cap $c_\ell$ is active, and otherwise equals the request. The resulting correction is $\mathbf{r}_{j,\ell}=s_{\mathrm{app}}(p_j,\ell)A^\ell_\phi(\mathbf{h}_{j,\ell},\mathbf{G})$; its \emph{post-scale residual magnitude} is $m_{j,\ell}=\|\mathbf{r}_{j,\ell}\|_2$. Thus neither a requested nor an applied scale label alone determines the correction magnitude.

In the evaluated up-only Fresh C configuration, the GeoTex wrappers on UNet up blocks $\texttt{up\_0}$, $\texttt{up\_1}$ and $\texttt{up\_2}$ are assigned to the deep, middle and shallow cap groups, respectively. These names identify the implementation's grouped injection points; they do not assert universal semantic roles for denoising stages. The corresponding Fresh C caps are 3.0, 3.5, and 0.8. The shallow requests in all three C3 phases, 1.25/2.50/1.25, are applied as 0.80/0.80/0.80; deep and middle retain the requested profile. These caps belong to the existing wrapper and are not introduced by TCAS. Their values and group names do not establish causal semantic roles. GFL and GFH also clip at the shallow cap. These values describe the evaluated Fresh C wrapper; row-level cap semantics for the historical 24-object probe are not recoverable. TCAS scales a geometry residual added to feature activations; it does not reweight conditional and unconditional score predictions as classifier-free guidance does.

\subsection{Image-Space Metrics and Texture-Variation Diagnostics}
\label{subsec:metrics}

The endpoints describe different outcomes. \emph{Image-space agreement} refers to target-view PSNR, LPIPS, and SSIM measurements on the named image regions; it is not a measure of final 3D material quality. \emph{Reference-color agreement} refers to color differences such as CIEDE2000 computed against the corresponding reference. \emph{Texture variation} refers to statistics such as RGB standard deviation, Laplacian variance, gradient magnitude, and high-frequency energy; these may rise with detail or artifacts and are not fidelity measures. \emph{Subjective preference} refers only to the submitted forced-choice study. \emph{Baked 3D appearance} refers to rendered mesh outputs after texture projection or baking, while \emph{PBR/material fidelity} additionally concerns appearance under the material and illumination model. These endpoints are not interchangeable. FG-SSIM, Edge-SSIM, and Crop-SSIM quantify structural similarity on the indicated image regions; PSNR measures pixel error; LPIPS compares learned image features \cite{wang2004ssim,zhang2018lpips}.

Laplacian variance, RGB standard deviation, and gradient magnitude can describe local high-frequency or color variation. For example, a foreground Laplacian statistic may be written as
\begin{equation}
M_{\mathrm{var}}=\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\operatorname{Lap}(I_{\mathrm{out}}\odot G_{\mathrm{mask}})\right),
\end{equation}
where $G_{\mathrm{mask}}$ is the foreground mask. A larger value is not necessarily better: detail, noise, repeated patterns, or artifacts can all increase local variation. We therefore do not interpret these proxies as texture fidelity or use them alone to claim that a schedule better preserves reference appearance.

\subsection{TCAS Control Semantics and Comparison Design}
\label{subsec:tcas}

TCAS replaces a globally fixed requested adapter scale with a piecewise profile over normalized sampling progress. The early/middle/late partition is an operational description, not a universal claim about the function of every denoising stage. C3's low--high--low profile is the recorded development choice evaluated in this study; it is not presented as an independently derived optimum.

To avoid ambiguity caused by different raw timestep directions in different schedulers, $p_j=j/(T-1)$ denotes execution progress for inference step $j\in\{0,\ldots,T-1\}$. Thus $p=0$ is the first update from the initial noisy latent and $p=1$ is the last denoising update before decoding. For $T=50$, TCAS divides execution into steps 0--16, 17--32, and 33--49 (17/16/17 steps):
\begin{equation}
s_{\mathrm{req}}(p) = \begin{cases} s_e, & 0 \leq p < 1/3 \\ s_m, & 1/3 \leq p < 2/3 \\ s_l, & 2/3 \leq p \leq 1 \end{cases}.
\label{eq:tcas}
\end{equation}

We call this variation across the 50 inference steps the \emph{temporal allocation}. A \emph{layer allocation} varies the requested scale across the three wrapper groups at a fixed step. For a schedule with applied values $s_{\mathrm{app}}(j,\ell)$, its per-group applied-scale sum is $B_\ell=\sum_j s_{\mathrm{app}}(j,\ell)$; the unweighted sum $B_{\mathrm{nom}}=\sum_\ell B_\ell$ is a \emph{nominal applied-scale budget}. It does not weight scales by adapter activations. We log post-scale correction summaries, including wrapper-level $L_2$ norm and mean absolute entry. The three wrapper groups operate on feature maps with different spatial dimensions, and the traces do not retain channel counts; raw $L_2$ values are therefore not used for cross-layer dose comparisons. The per-entry mean absolute correction reported in Supplementary Section~S1 is a dimension-normalized descriptive summary, not an equal-dose intervention. Correction magnitudes also depend on the current hidden state and earlier denoising updates, so equal nominal budgets do not imply equal realized corrections.

The selected C3 configuration used in this paper is:
\begin{equation}
(s_e, s_m, s_l) = (1.25, 2.50, 1.25).
\end{equation}

The low--high--low profile is a concrete inference-time intervention motivated by the submitted development analysis. Its stage interpretation is a task-specific hypothesis: the tested early, middle, and late allocations may produce different responses. Fresh C evaluates the recorded profile against matched controls but does not prove a universal function for any stage. TCAS adds no trained parameters and does not modify base-model or adapter weights; it replaces the requested scale during inference.

\paragraph{What C3 changes and what its comparisons identify}
C3 requests $(1.25,2.50,1.25)$ for each of the 50 steps across the three layer groups before native caps. In Fresh C, the applied deep and middle profiles retain those values; the shallow profile is clipped to 0.8 throughout. GFL requests 1.25 at every step and group, so its deep and middle values are 1.25 throughout and its shallow value is also clipped to 0.8. Their per-group applied-scale sums are therefore 82.5 versus 62.5 in deep and middle, and 40 for both in shallow. The C3--GFL contrast changes the middle-third deep/middle scale magnitude as well as its temporal allocation; it is not a timing-only comparison.

To identify a temporal-placement contrast, controls hold the per-layer applied values and nominal per-group sums fixed while moving the same values between time windows. A scale-strength contrast instead changes amplitude while preserving layer and temporal allocation. Matching requested values or applied-scale sums does not match the evolving residual corrections; raw $L_2$ values are also dimension-sensitive across wrapper groups and cannot establish cross-layer dose matching. The 300-object allocation comparison matches applied sums in each layer group and therefore estimates an allocation effect at a fixed scalar budget, not a residual-dose-independent mechanism. The historical-request sensitivity panel is reported separately.

\paragraph{Development origin and scope of C3}
The submitted study records C3 $=(1.25,2.50,1.25)$ as the selected profile from a 24-object development probe containing 17 uniform, layer-wise, and time-conditioned variants. The available historical records do not preserve the row-level outputs needed to reconstruct that ranking. We therefore treat C3 as the recorded operating point and make no optimality or uniqueness claim based on the original probe. Fresh C evaluates it without further schedule search.

\subsection{Geometry Adapter and Inference Interface}
\label{subsec:adapterimpl}

The pretrained GeoTex-Adapter receives a five-channel input comprising a three-channel normal map, a one-channel depth map, and a one-channel foreground mask. Its multi-scale encoder produces residual features that are injected into the frozen UNet. TCAS acts on the requested multiplier along denoising progress; it does not retrain the adapter, the UNet, or the VAE and introduces no learned parameters.

Visual comparisons use recorded outputs with shared object framing and crops. They illustrate task behavior and appearance limitations; quantitative conclusions use the stated object-level metrics.

\subsection{Temporal Allocation at Matched Applied-Scale Budgets}
\label{subsec:matched-budget300}
Early-, middle-, and late-high move the same sixteen high steps to updates 0--15, 17--32, and 34--49. Deep/middle low and high are 1.25 and 2.50; shallow stays at its 0.8 cap. Fixed Mean uses 1.65 before the cap. Each allocation therefore has per-group sums $(82.5,82.5,40)$. All six conditions share the object inputs and seed, giving 1,800 paired object-condition rows.

The equal-budget addendum's internally specified primary question is Middle-high minus the objectwise average of Early-high and Late-high FG-PSNR. Its mean is $-0.0339$ dB (10,000-resample pointwise object-bootstrap interval $[-0.1172,+0.0514]$; two-sided 20,000-draw sign-flip $p=0.4324$), with 119/300 objects positive. The median is $-0.2296$ dB. This result does not support a middle-window advantage and does not establish equivalence. It was specified before the addendum's new allocations, after earlier Fresh C outcomes were known; it is not an independent prospective confirmation. The primary, twelve-test secondary family and later descriptive all-pair analysis are distinguished in Supplementary Section~S2.

C3 minus Fixed Mean is $-0.133$ dB (pointwise interval $[-0.170,-0.096]$); C3 minus Late-high is $-0.564$ dB ($[-0.634,-0.494]$). C3 also trails both on LPIPS, FG-SSIM and Edge-SSIM. It improves over Early-high by approximately 0.50 dB. Within these six conditions Fixed High wins FG-PSNR on 172 objects, Late-high on 64, and C3 on none; this is an endpoint-specific count, not a universal quality ranking.

A descriptive four-endpoint comparison complements these winner counts. On 270/300 objects, at least one of the other five controls weakly dominates C3 on all four recorded composite endpoints and improves at least one. Fixed High alone does so on 173 objects, but C3 dominates it on 16; their remaining 111 objects have competing endpoints. This post-inspection, point-estimate analysis is not statistical separation on each object or a ranking of unmeasured material quality. Full counts are in Supplementary Section~S2.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Strength and timing answer different questions.}
A low--high--low curve can appear effective when the fixed comparator is weak, yet that contrast changes both amplitude and placement. Fresh C makes this distinction concrete: the C3 gain over Fixed Low increases deep/middle applied sums from 62.5 to 82.5, whereas Fixed High and Generic Linear have better means on the four image endpoints. The equal-budget primary does not support the earlier middle-window advantage. Separate pairwise results favor Fixed Mean and Late-high over C3 on several endpoints, while Late-high trades lower LPIPS and higher PSNR against lower FG-SSIM than Fixed Mean. These observations change the tested recommendation, but do not show that every temporal schedule is ineffective or that Late-high is universally best.

\paragraph{Matching scalar sums does not identify a residual-dose mechanism.}
The native shallow cap makes all three C3 requests identical after clipping in that group. A requested-profile comparison can therefore describe an intervention that was never applied as drawn. Reporting the implemented per-group profiles and sums removes that ambiguity. Matching those sums still leaves the adapter activation and hidden-state trajectory free to change. The matched-budget contrasts estimate the total effect of allocating the scalar control, including the resulting correction changes; they do not isolate timing at an equal realized residual dose. Raw correction norms also depend on tensor dimensions. The descriptive activation traces in Supplementary Section~S1 cannot validate a universal stage mechanism or assign a structural/texture function to each cap group.

\paragraph{What baking adds to the interpretation.}
Generated-view metrics are informative: broad within-object rankings are positively associated with baked rankings. However, projection and texture construction add losses, and the minimum-score control can change between image and asset endpoints. The association weakens in GSO when restricted to closely competing scale/time controls. These are associations and changes of point-estimate winners, not statistically resolved rank reversals.

In the compatible GeoTex pilot, Late-high reduces LPIPS by about 2\% relative to Fixed Mean but closes only 3.7\% of its gap to the matched GT bake. The latter is a reference for projection/resampling loss, not a theoretical ceiling; GT error is not subtracted from method errors. Reference-color and detail discrepancies remain visible, and the separate GSO protocol does not detect the same allocation benefit. Thus the asset evidence quantifies a local control effect alongside the much larger remaining appearance problem. Incoming-view disagreement is also a distinct diagnostic: a constant wrong color can have zero variance. Neither this diagnostic nor unlit LPIPS certifies seams, physical albedo or PBR/material fidelity.

\paragraph{What can and cannot transfer.}
The native MV-Adapter50 and GSO100 primary intervals do not detect a middle-window advantage; they do not establish equivalence. The secondary GSO late-window PSNR effect is small and has no detected nonreference LPIPS counterpart. A reference-conditioned GeoTex checkpoint gives a different Late--Mean direction, and MVDiffusion tests only a partial correspondence operator. These observations limit a portable middle-stage rule. Because inputs, weights, operators and sampling/projection protocols change together, the studies cannot attribute their response differences to a backbone or domain alone. Equal numerical scale labels across them are not common physical doses.

\paragraph{Consequences for evaluating a texturing control.}
A practical comparison should first retain the deployed input and camera contract and compare native/default and competitive fixed settings. To interpret placement, it should report the cap-adjusted profiles and match per-group applied sums, with residual traces used to describe rather than certify dose. Reference-color errors and full-object displays should accompany image metrics. Actual baking with shared meshes, unseen cameras and a matched reference bake can then assess whether the effect matters for the asset. Finally, fixed-seed differences should be examined at object level: positive cohort means can coexist with mixed directions across seeds. This is an evaluation sequence supported by the observed failure of stronger interpretations, not a newly validated selection algorithm. The evidence gives no general reason to replace a deployment's fixed setting with C3.

\paragraph{Selection, statistics and replication limits.}
The bake cohorts favor compatible native-UV assets and GSO uses a source-ordered convenience sample. The two bake studies have different conditioning/projection protocols; the revised GSO calibration followed inspection of initial outcomes. Equal-budget analyses were specified on an already evaluated Fresh C cohort and remain retrospective; the dominance, seed and coverage addenda are descriptive. Separate multiplicity families and pointwise intervals are not one overall error guarantee. Views and seeds remain nested within object, and three fixed seeds do not estimate training-checkpoint variation.

Fresh C uses controlled retraining and sampling rather than the authenticated historical main checkpoint or the complete default pipeline. The original $+0.96$ dB result is not reproduced, and the historical-request panel has an unknown substituted input, precluding a verified 276-object holdout interpretation. Original preference evidence concerns its own stimuli; the historical FAC manifest and weights are incomplete. These limits are retained in Supplementary Sections~S6--S8. They constrain historical replication and deployment claims while leaving the paired within-protocol comparisons interpretable.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Geometry-control allocation in multi-view texturing must be interpreted at the implemented interface and the final asset endpoint. On the held-out GeoTex cohort, competitive baselines and cap-adjusted budgets change the interpretation of C3's gain over Fixed Low. The specified matched-budget primary does not support a middle-window advantage, while separate pairwise contrasts reveal endpoint-dependent placement effects. Native-UV baking measures a small Late-high benefit on ten compatible objects together with persistent appearance error; a separate GSO study does not detect this benefit. Native MV-Adapter comparisons also do not establish the proposed middle-window gain. The resulting knowledge is a bounded account of what these control comparisons identify and how their outcomes relate to textured meshes. It supports stronger comparison and asset-evaluation decisions, without a universal schedule, independently derived stage mechanism or faithful material reproduction.
