Response to the Reviewers
Manuscript: CAG-D-26-00962
Title: Timestep-Conditioned Adapter Scaling for Multi-view Diffusion Texture Generation: Control Allocation
and Baked Appearance
Dear Editor and Reviewers,
This manuscript originated from Paper 75 recommended by CAD/Graphics 2026 for Computers & Graphics. We
thank the reviewers for the initial conference comments and the subsequent journal review. The response below
addresses the actual second-round concerns, followed by a compact record of continuity with the first round.
The revised paper studies the historical TCAS/C3 profile as an inference-time intervention on an existing
GeoTex geometry-residual interface. Its scientific contribution is a controlled account of what a geometry-control
comparison identifies and how it relates to baked appearance. We distinguish requests, inherited wrapper-capped
applied scales and state-dependent residual corrections; report competitive fixed and linear controls; preserve the
declared matched-budget primary; and assess selected native-UV bakes and separate native conditioning interfaces.
The evidence revises earlier schedule recommendations rather than establishing a universally preferred method.
Remaining historical-reproduction, deployment and methodological-novelty limits are stated below.

Reviewer 1 — second round
Comment 1: Color shifts, full-object comparisons, native pipeline and competitive controls, baked
unseen views, seams and video
Changes and evidence. Complete objects and matched pixel crops are presented in Main Figure 2, p. 7 and
Main Figure 3, p. 8; actual source colors and appearance defects are preserved. Fresh C includes competitive Fixed
High and Generic Linear alongside the original C3/Fixed Low comparison and matched-budget allocations. The
revised Figure 1 identifies separate reference and geometry routes, the TCAS request intervention and inherited
native caps before feature addition. The illustrative schematic depicts the control interface and downstream
workflow; its image panels are not used as experimental evidence. This improves method explanation without
supplying new evidence of fidelity.
The asset assessment exports native-UV meshes and averages eleven unseen unlit renders within each object,
alongside matched GT-256 self-bakes. GeoTex10 Late-high minus Fixed Mean FG-LPIPS is −0.002652 (95%
object-bootstrap interval [−0.004144,−0.001367]; exact sign-flip p=0.00195). The separate GSO20 difference is
+0.00008379 (interval [−0.00010305,+0.00028592]; original-family Holm p=0.78765), which does not detect the
same benefit. The GeoTex video presents two input-order, rights-cleared objects at eleven discrete unseen cameras.
A separate GSO video illustrates one matched object under its recorded conditions; neither video estimates
population quality.
Locations. Main Figure 1, p. 4; Main Figure 2, p. 7; Main Figure 3, p. 8; Main Table 2, p. 6; Main Section 3.5, p. 5;
Main Section 4.4, p. 8; Main Table 4, p. 8; Main Table 5, p. 10; Main Figure 4, p. 10; Supplement Sections S4–S6/S9;
separate Supplementary_GeoTex_Baked_Comparison.mp4 and Supplementary_GSO_Baked_Comparison.mp4.
Remaining limitation. The controlled Fresh C setting lacks an input- and seed-matched complete-default/native
GeoTex quality comparison. Native/unset residual and capped Constant 1.0 controls do not equal that complete
pipeline. The bake cohorts are small and compatibility-selected; unfilled coverage and sampling/UV limitations
remain, and no validated seam-quality endpoint or PBR/material-fidelity result is claimed. Full-object displays
and the video illustrate errors, not their prevalence.

Comment 2: Same-protocol held-out comparison of the original main adapter and baselines
Changes and evidence. Fresh C contains 300 UIDs disjoint from the historical selection/evaluation pool and
recorded training lists. Conditions share a frozen retrained checkpoint, inputs, targets and paired seeds. Its
original C3−Fixed Low FG-PSNR primary is +0.522 dB (95% object-bootstrap interval [+0.404,+0.641]; 206/300
favorable). Strong fixed and linear controls are reported on this same cohort. The paper explicitly separates this
controlled retraining from numerical reproduction of the historical +0.96 dB result.

Comment 3: Texture variation versus faithful texture reproduction
Changes and evidence. We separate target-view image agreement, reference-color agreement, variation
diagnostics, historical preference and actual baked appearance. Gradients, RGB standard deviation and Laplacian
variance may increase through detail or artifacts and are not fidelity measures. The 26-object color diagnosis is
failure-enriched and has no prevalence interpretation. Complete-object displays explicitly preserve the discrepancy
between generated patterns and reference appearance. The asset results contextualize the small GeoTex bake
change relative to the matched GT reference bake; substantial error remains.
Locations. Main Section 3.2, p. 3; Main Section 4.3, p. 6; Main Figure 3, p. 8; Main Section 4.4, p. 8; Main
Section 5, p. 12; Supplement Sections S5–S7.
Remaining limitation. Unlit base-color scores and variation proxies do not establish faithful materials or
appearance under changing illumination. We do not replace that missing validation with better diagram presentation
or the historical preference result.

Comment 4: Paired intervals, Edge-SSIM and non-inferiority/equivalence
Changes and evidence. Validated cohorts use paired object-level intervals and retain Edge-SSIM. C3−Fixed
High Edge-SSIM is −0.01881 (95% interval [−0.02083,−0.01678]; frozen Holm p=0.00059); foreground SSIM is
−0.04553 (interval approximately [−0.04937,−0.04165]). These losses remain visible in the competitive-control
results. We state that an interval crossing zero does not establish equivalence or non-inferiority. The 276-slot
diagnostics have a separate, explicitly weaker identity.
Locations. Main Table 2, p. 6; Main Section 4.1, p. 5; Main Section 4.2, p. 5; Main Section 3.5, p. 5; Supplement
Section S2 and Supplement Table S2, p. 5; Section S7 for requested-slot sensitivity.
Remaining limitation. No non-inferiority margin was prespecified. Pointwise intervals and separate correction
families do not provide one global experiment-wide guarantee; non-detection is not equivalence.

Comment 5: FAC reproducibility and release of code
Changes and evidence. We retain the seven previously reported FAC configurations and their adverse FG-
PSNR changes (−1.10 to −2.94 dB relative to TCAS). Recovered controller code, entry points and bounded
execution/source records are supplied; the twelve-object untrained comparison remains a separate reported
historical analysis. Component checks exercise synthetic tensors and state serialization rather than the complete
historical diffusion training run.
Locations. Main Section 4.6, p. 11; Supplement Section S7; Supplement Table S30, p. 50; recovered-source and
component-validation records in the accompanying evidence package.
Remaining limitation. The submitted training pool states 1,706 objects, whereas a recovered configuration
lists 1,200; run-bound training identities, seeds and historical weights remain incomplete. We do not claim exact
full-model replay or reclassify those historical negative results as newly confirmed experiments.

Concern 1: The methodological contribution remains limited
The revision addresses the controlled empirical question and its graphics relevance; the limitation in methodological
novelty remains.
Changes and distinction from prior work. We agree that adding a temporal scale profile does not establish
a new general scheduling method. The official ControlNet depth-to-image demo assigns a fixed thirteen-block
profile in guess mode according to block index; it is not adaptive to the depth-map contents. Diffusers v0.21.0
exposes start/end temporal gates. SSI is a closer joint layer/time precedent: it varies StyleID query blending and
separately schedules geometric ControlNet conditioning. The revised Related Work distinguishes these mechanisms
and credits those precedents. Official ControlNet source, versioned Diffusers documentation, SSI original paper.
TCAS changes the requested scale on the existing GeoTex residual path. Native caps belong to the evaluated
wrapper, not to a new TCAS clipping design. The applied multiplier is the minimum of the request and that
group cap before addition to frozen features. The resulting correction still depends on the evolving state. This
identifies the tested operator; it does not add a learned branch, selector, objective or general theory.
Evidence-backed contribution and graphics relevance. The revised study establishes what can be concluded
about this geometry-control setting that a C3-versus-weak-fixed comparison alone could not establish. C3’s +0.522
dB foreground-PSNR gain changes deep/middle applied sums from 62.5 to 82.5 each, while shallow remains 40. It
therefore cannot identify an advantage from temporal placement alone. Strong Fixed High and Generic Linear
controls have better means on the four reported Fresh C endpoints. At matched per-group applied sums, the
preserved timing primary Middle−average(Early,Late) is −0.0338775 dB (95% interval [−0.117170,+0.051422],
p=0.432378), which does not detect the proposed middle-window advantage. The later Middle−Fixed Mean
(−0.13326 dB) and Middle−Late-high (−0.56395 dB) comparisons remain separate; neither replaces that primary.
For graphics researchers the output of interest is a textured asset, and the generated RGB views are intermediate
products. Selected native-UV bakes with matched ground-truth self-bakes and eleven unseen cameras quantify the
appearance effect under fixed evaluation protocols. The local GeoTex10 LPIPS gain is small relative to remaining
error and is not detected in the separate GSO20 study. Native conditioning-interface comparisons further limit
treating a shared scale label as a transferable dose. These findings correct the earlier C3 recommendation and
identify when a control comparison can support a timing interpretation or an asset-quality claim. Their value
rests on this controlled task knowledge, rather than experimental volume or statistical significance alone.
Locations. Main Section 1, p. 1; Main Section 2.3, p. 2; Main Section 3.1, p. 3; Main Section 3.3, p. 3; Main
Table 2, p. 6; Main Section 4.2, p. 5; Main Table 5, p. 10; Main Table 6, p. 10; Main Table 7, p. 11; Main Section
5, p. 12; Supplement Sections S1–S5.
Remaining limitation. TCAS remains a manually specified intervention on an existing adapter. The equal-
budget addendum reuses previously inspected Fresh C objects and is not independent prospective confirmation;
equal applied sums do not establish equal residual dose. Cohort, interface and projection differences prevent causal
assignment of the varying effects, and unlit rendering does not establish material fidelity. The evidence supports
task-specific controlled empirical understanding, not a universal scheduling method, optimum or transfer result.
We acknowledge that this does not eliminate the reviewer’s methodological novelty concern.

Concern 2: CAI formalizes a selected schedule rather than deriving it
Changes and evidence. The independent CAI derivation and optimality claims are removed. C3 is described
as the recorded choice from the historical 24-object/17-variant development probe and carried forward without a
new schedule search. One-third windows are operational partitions of sampling progress, not a universal semantic
decomposition. The preserved matched-budget primary does not detect the proposed middle-window advantage.
Locations. Main Section 3.3, p. 3; Main Section 4.2, p. 5; Main Section 5, p. 12; Supplement Section S7,
Supplement S7 (C3 Development Record), p. 52 and Supplement Table S33, p. 54.
Remaining limitation. Historical candidate-level records cannot reconstruct the original ranking. C3 is neither
independently derived nor verified as unique or optimal; no causal stage-utility theory is claimed.

Concern 3: Missing cross-backbone validation
Changes and evidence. A native-camera MV-Adapter comparison evaluates fifty input-selected objects with
seven shared conditions. Its specified Middle−Fixed Mean foreground-PSNR primary is +0.029 dB (95% object
interval [−0.019,+0.081]; paired p=0.281; 29/50 favorable). A separate GSO100 study uses this same native
interface on different inputs and does not detect its specified middle-window advantage. The retained 75-object
MVDiffusion study scales a partial correspondence-block operator while leaving depth concatenation unscaled. We
report their genuine, endpoint-dependent results separately and do not pool them as equal-dose replications.
Locations. Main Section 4.5, p. 9; Main Table 6, p. 10; Main Figure 5, p. 11; Main Table 7, p. 11; Main Figure 6,
p. 12; Supplement Sections S3/S4/S8 and Supplement Table S15, p. 18.
Remaining limitation. Native MV-Adapter is a different conditioning-interface test, not an architecture-
matched replication of GeoTex residual scaling. GSO100 is not another backbone, and MVDiffusion tests a partial
operator. The studies expand empirical scope without establishing a general cross-backbone schedule advantage;
the reviewer’s broad generalization concern remains unresolved.
