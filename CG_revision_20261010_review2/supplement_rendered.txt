This supplement supports the revised 01549 manuscript. Fresh C is an image-space comparison on 300
UIDs disjoint from the original 01549 evaluation/probe IDs, the 1,118-object training list, and clean-v2
evaluation IDs. Its primary conditions and same-cohort addendum share one frozen adapter checkpoint,
runner source, seed policy, inputs, target views, and metric implementation; the addendum is not an
independent replication. Fresh C uses a controlled-retraining checkpoint and is not presented as a numerical
reproduction of historical Table 4. Historical cohorts and interface checks are reported separately. Object
is the inferential unit; multiple views and wrapper records do not increase N .

S5. Historical and cross-interface boundaries
The completed MAIN-STAGE-276 follow-up contains 1,104 records (276 objects by four schedules), with 50
Euler steps, seed 42, unique-six target views, shared initial latents, and a common runner within the run.
Its final run manifest records completion with exit code 0. The follow-up was specified after inspection of
earlier clean-v2 results and was not blind. It is reported separately from Fresh C and is not an independent
replication of the original 01549 development probe.
Here, temporal L–L–H means that the requested values are low in early and middle steps and high
in late steps. This is distinct from the layer-conditioned Fresh C LLH addendum condition described in
the main paper. With 50 steps, the code assigns 17/16/17 steps to early/middle/late. C3 therefore has
deep/middle applied-scale sums of 82.5; temporal L–L–H has 83.75 because its high stage occupies 17
rather than 16 steps; the shallow applied sum is 40 for both after the 0.8 cap. The fixed-mean comparator
has a deep/middle sum of 83.33 and a shallow sum of 40. These are near-matched scalar sums, not exactly
equal sums or matched residual-norm doses.
Table S7 gives all six formal paired endpoints for C3 versus temporal L–L–H and C3 versus the fixedmean comparator. Effects are C3 minus comparator; positive values favor C3 for PSNR/SSIM, and negative
values favor C3 for LPIPS. Temporal L–L–H outperforms C3 on all six formal endpoints in this postinspection stage-placement experiment: it has higher PSNR/SSIM and lower LPIPS endpoints. In contrast,
C3 exceeds fixed-mean on five of six formal endpoints. These results show that the measured response
varies with tested stage placement; they do not establish a universal schedule winner. The contrasts do
not isolate placement from the small applied-scale-sum differences or the resulting residual-state trajectory,
and post-scale residual norms are not matched.

The frozen MV-Adapter layer/window probe analyzed 98 of 99 planned objects; the omitted input was a
GLB with path geometry and no triangle faces, so no substitute was used. Its production additive-residual
bootstrap had an invalid null and is not used for interaction inference. The post hoc object-cluster robust
Wald reanalysis found no interaction surviving its exploratory four-metric Holm correction (FG-LPIPS
Holm p = 0.303; PSNR p = 0.868; FG-SSIM p = 0.156; CIEDE2000 p = 0.862). This does not establish
absence of interaction or equivalence.
A separate 76-object Exact-holdout MV-Adapter panel adds layer-wise fixed, L–HL, and L–LH outputs
to existing global controls. It scales condition-guider features before UNet down-block residual injection
and does not reproduce the GeoTex ResNet residual interface. The wrapper matched the existing global
runner bitwise in a 3-object identity audit, but this is not an identity comparison against the unmodified
official pipeline at native scale. Table S10 gives the seven condition means and Table S11 gives all five
paired contrasts across six endpoints. Layer-wise/global mean differences are metric-dependent; object
medians and favorable fractions also vary. Within-profile temporal comparisons show one pointwise interval
excluding zero in each comparison, with the other five including zero. These 30 intervals are unadjusted
and descriptive. The protocol and result files first appear in one preserved commit, so we classify this
analysis as retrospective sensitivity rather than confirmation. It establishes neither equivalence nor transfer
of TCAS.

weight checks and integer-period translation invariance. Scalar coverage masks are read without an RGB
transfer function; decoding grayscale coverage as sRGB would change the foreground threshold. GT source
images are resized to 256 using bicubic RGB and the same nearest-index alpha masks as generated sources;

unmatched-resolution diagnostic.

All 300 Fresh C inputs were screened; old five-object outputs and the previously inspected REPEAT
diagnostic object were excluded. Eligibility required available source GLB, one opaque white-factor
base-color material, native TEXCOORD_0, no vertex colors, at most 300,000 triangles, and source
camera-mask alignment passing the frozen gate. This input-selected set is smaller than the target twenty
and below fifteen; we retain the pilot designation and all ten outcomes. No failed or adverse-quality object
was replaced. Input-audit exclusions included 157 objects outside the single-OPAQUE-material domain,
27 nonwhite base-color factors, 21 missing native UVs, 20 vertex-color assets and one non-REPEAT
sampler. Seventy-four passed that material/input check; later gates excluded 13 oversized meshes and 46
camera-mask alignment failures, with previously baked objects excluded separately. These restrictions
describe the current preparation/evaluation implementation, not TCAS failure rates or intrinsic asset
quality. Seven conditions generated 70 object-condition rows; eight bake conditions including GT generated
80 artifacts, with 880 unseen renders and 480 source-camera renders.
The frozen primary was Late-high–Fixed Mean FG-LPIPS. C3–GFH PSNR, LPIPS, SSIM and
CIEDE2000 form one four-test secondary Holm family. All other complete pairs are descriptive. Views are
averaged before inference. Intervals use 50,000 paired object resamples and are pointwise, not simultaneous.
The ten-object primary uses all 210 sign flips; the secondary family uses the same exact test. A sign-flip
test assumes symmetry/exchangeability of paired differences under its null; an interval alone is not a
practical-equivalence criterion. No non-inferiority margin was chosen.
