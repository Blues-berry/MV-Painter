\section{S4. Bounded layer-by-window map and dose limits}

A3b covers 15 layer-by-window interventions relative to the all-low reference. The cluster-Wald and GEE tests detect response heterogeneity (FG-LPIPS interaction RMSE 0.000342; FG-PSNR 0.0205 dB). The dose calibration target was missed: deep and middle were approximately 20\% below target, while shallow was 98.98\% above target. The adjusted three-layer dose model retains 0/2,250 rows in common support. Therefore the response-surface test is not interpreted as a dose-independent mechanism. A2 is discovery evidence and is not used as an independent confirmatory replication.

\section{S5. GLB-native evaluation and visual audit}

The stored GLBs were rendered with their embedded glTF base-color sampler and frozen camera protocol. Source GLB hashes (160) and rendered image hashes (1,760) passed. Each object mean pools 11 unseen views. No texture was rebaked. The endpoint is unlit base-color and does not establish full-PBR appearance. Four objects without UVs were excluded before baking, so strict N=24 3D coverage is unsupported. The old seam analyzer is not treated as valid evidence for the exported mapping; no seam claim is made.

The 24-object 2D visual cohort was fixed using ground-truth-only strata before method outcomes. Each panel compares the same six target views for ground truth and seven generated conditions, including no adapter and fixed-scale controls. The panels are a completeness-oriented visual record; they are not a blinded human rating. Manual inspection recorded material/color mismatch in 18/24 objects and fine-detail loss in 21/24. \section{S7. Cross-interface results}

The MV-Adapter experiment evaluates 98 objects from 99 planned across a bounded one-window additive-residual intervention. No global interaction survives the exploratory four-metric correction; isolated late shallow LPIPS changes are small and do not establish a general null or a transfer law.

MVDiffusion is a separate 75-object result on the unchanged SD2-depth compatibility deployment base. Its schedule interpolates serial correspondence-aware CPBlock outputs, not an independent geometry-residual branch. For layer-LLH versus global fixed-low, paired means trade structural/color endpoints against GT-relative texture error: PSNR is -0.454 dB, FG-SSIM -0.0194, Edge-SSIM -0.0059, while CIEDE2000 is -1.373; FG-LPIPS is not separated. This is a boundary for that interface and intervention, not a matched causal architecture comparison. Absolute scores are never pooled across these cohorts.

\section{S8. Human preference and scope}

The original 01549 3AFC study is retained as complementary perceptual-preference evidence for its original conditions only. It included 24 participants and 30 objects; each criterion had 720 choices. As reported in the submitted study, the uniform-scale 1.25 condition received 46.1\% of texture-naturalness choices, uniform-scale 2.50 received 46.0\% of shape-consistency choices, and C3/TCAS received 58.1\% of overall-quality choices (reported 95\% participant-by-object cluster-bootstrap CI [50.1\%, 65.8\%]). This pattern supports a preferred balance among those three conditions, not universal superiority or transfer to LLH. The raw vote-level file was unavailable for an independent reanalysis; the reported counts and interval are therefore identified as results from the submitted 01549 study.

We analyzed the prespecified 40-slot human-preference study of LLH versus GFL, GFH, GC3, and generic linear. The appearance question asked which output better matched the reference object's visible appearance, including color and material; the texture question asked about texture naturalness. Forty slots were represented. We retained 37 field-valid participants (two failed the recorded comprehension check and one response was incomplete), covering 24 objects and eight comparison-by-question endpoints. For each endpoint, judgments were scored as LLH choice = 1, comparator choice = 0, tie = 0.5; object means were equally weighted, and uncertainty was estimated with a two-way participant/object cluster bootstrap (10,000 resamples, fixed seed 20261005). The eight pre-specified endpoints formed one Holm family. Pointwise 95\% intervals are shown; the number of judgments is 222 per endpoint. This is confirmatory for the planned endpoint family, with the execution deviations below reported in full.

Although all eight point estimates exceed 0.5, none provides multiplicity-adjusted evidence that participants preferred LLH. In particular, the generic-linear intervals cross 0.5; absent an equivalence margin and with the intervals spanning both directions, these results do not establish equivalence or non-inferiority. The four comparisons and eight-endpoint analysis were specified before collection, so we report the endpoint analysis as confirmatory with documented execution deviations. None of the 40 uploaded task schedules matches a schedule in the frozen assignment package; left/right orientation is fixed within all 96 object-by-comparison cells across participants, although LLH is left for 12 and right for 12 objects in each comparator; and the export lacks a randomization seed, question order, raw comprehension answers, and a stimulus-alias-to-frozen-image hash crosswalk. The response export also lacks the coordinator's completed/withdrawn closeout log and ethics/consent documentation. These deviations limit verification of randomization, stimulus identity, stopping, and study governance; they do not justify discarding the prespecified endpoints or selectively retaining results. The study measures relative judgments of fixed 2D outputs and does not establish absolute reference fidelity, unseen-view quality, or 3D-bake quality.

The aggregate endpoint table, forest plot, analysis provenance, source-commit record, and executable analysis script are included under \texttt{evidence/human\_study/}. The four anonymous participant-level CSVs were previously uploaded at the source branch and commit recorded in the provenance file; this package does not copy them again. Their continued public availability and the missing consent/ethics documentation remain separate data-governance items.
